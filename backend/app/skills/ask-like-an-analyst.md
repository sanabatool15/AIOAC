# Ask like an analyst

Before looking at columns, find out what the answer will be used for. Pick the ONE question that matters most.

## Questions worth asking
- What decision will this change? "If you knew which customers might leave, what would you do with that?"
- What is one row? One customer, one order, one visit? The answer must be about that thing.
- When is each column known? A column filled in only after the outcome (a cancel date, a closing note) would give the answer away. Ask when it gets filled in.
- What counts as a yes? "Left" could mean cancelled, or just stopped buying.

## How to ask
1. Say what you already see: "This looks like one row per customer, with a column called Churn."
2. Ask one question, with the reason: "Is Churn filled in before or after the customer decides? I ask because a column known only afterwards would make the test look better than real life."
3. State your assumption and move on if they are unsure: "I will assume it is known beforehand; tell me if not."

## Do not
- Do not ask several questions at once, or a question the data already answers.
- Do not stall. One question, one assumption, then carry on.
