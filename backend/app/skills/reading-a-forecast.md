# Reading a forecast

A forecast is a guess from a small free test, not a promise.

## Say these parts
1. The estimate: "On the rows I tested, I think a full run would get about N out of 100 right."
2. The band: "It could land anywhere from about A to B." A wide band means more doubt; say that.
3. The confidence: "I think there is about a C in 100 chance of reaching your target." Compare to a weather forecast: 70 in 100 rain still means dry days.
4. The comparison: next to the simple guess from the free test.

## If it is not eligible
- Give the plain reason first (too few rows, lopsided answer, weak columns).
- Offer the suggested target if the tool gave one: "I could honestly stand behind about N out of 100."
- Ask whether they would accept that, or want to look at the data first. A new target means a new free small test.

## Do not
- Do not choose the target for them.
- Do not call the estimate certain, or use words like guaranteed.
